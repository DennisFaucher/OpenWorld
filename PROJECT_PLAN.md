# OpenWorld: Project Plan

## Vision

A native macOS (Apple Silicon, Metal) third-person open-world game you can walk around, with the bright, saturated, stylized tropical-island look of an early-2000s platformer: low-poly geometry, simple textures, vivid water, sunny sky. All art and characters are **original** (no third-party/Nintendo assets). Target hardware: M4 Pro, 60-120 fps at native resolution with MSAA.

Tech stack: Swift 6 toolchain (language mode 5), Metal (runtime-compiled MSL), MetalKit, GameController, SwiftPM. See `AGENTS.md` for architecture and workflow.

## Status: v5.0 released — M1 (look and feel), M2 (character and movement), M3 (spray pack), M4 (world scale) and M5 (gameplay layer: crystals, a save file, islanders, synthesised sound and the shrine goal) complete apart from the deferred items in Known issues

Done and verified via headless renders (live input only smoke-tested):

- [x] SwiftPM executable, runtime-compiled shaders, windowed `MTKView` app with menu + full screen
- [x] Procedural island terrain (beach, grass, hills, mountain, plaza terrace), 240x240 mesh
- [x] Props: 80 palms, bushes, flowers, 5 plaza houses, bell tower
- [x] Sky shader (gradient, sun, procedural clouds) and distance fog
- [x] Water: waves, depth-based shallow/deep color, Fresnel sky reflection, sun glitter, animated shoreline foam, camera-centred grid out to 1500 units
- [x] Player: original character from sphere parts, walk cycle, blob shadow, wading, swimming (buoyancy)
- [x] Physics: gravity, jump, run, terrain snapping, world bounds, circle/AABB obstacle push-out
- [x] Orbit camera with mouse (captured), arrow keys, gamepad; collision against terrain and obstacles
- [x] Spray pack: water jet solved onto the eye-ray hit, splash on impact, wet patches that dry, aim pose, hover thrust on a refillable tank, washable goo splats, reticle and tank gauge
- [x] Headless `--shot` renderer for visual verification

## Known issues / rough edges

Fix opportunistically; none block the next milestones.

1. **Live input unverified.** Mouse capture, key handling, gamepad, and `swift run` window activation have not been exercised interactively. First task for anyone with a display: play it for a minute and fix whatever feels off (sensitivity, camera lag, jump feel, stuck keys).
2. ~~**Thin dark line at some shorelines**~~ (fixed). Root cause was the two-sided lighting hard-flipping the normal at `dot(N,V) = 0`, which lands exactly on the line where the ground is tangent to the view; it is now a smooth front/back blend. The shallows were also made to fade in gradually (alpha ramp + wider `shallow` falloff) instead of going opaque within ~1 m of depth.
3. ~~**Plaza terrace**~~ (fixed). The floor now ends on a modelled stone kerb ring (`World.buildProps`, 72 segments of three quads) plus a narrow stone border band and a contact shadow in the tile shader, so the checker stops on a deliberate edge instead of being cut off by grass. The mud-coloured slope is gone with the rock recolouring from the terrain item.
4. ~~**Shaded walls look tan/drab**~~ (fixed). The ambient is now a hemispheric three-colour blend with a per-face term (vertical faces facing the sun get a little more warmth), so a building's four walls no longer receive identical light. Contact occlusion is baked into the mesh: `MeshBuilder.groundedBox` ramps the wall colour darker over the lowest ~2.4 m through `quad4` per-corner colours.
5. ~~**Palm frond undersides are dark.**~~ (fixed). The fronds carry a translucency flag in `nrm.w`; `fs_main` adds `albedo * SUNCOL * saturate(-dot(Ng,V)) * saturate(dot(Ng,L)) * 0.55`, so looking up at a sunlit frond glows green instead of going black.
6. ~~Water vertices "swim"~~ — fixed by the clipmap (M4).
7. **Camera** can still clip into the player at its 2.2 m minimum distance when backed into a wall; props' collision is approximated by `topY`.
8. Player can end up inside an obstacle if spawned/teleported there (push-out is per-frame, not swept).
9. **Shadow map is re-rendered every frame** at 4096² (terrain + props + player; terrain and props are culled against the 90 m light box, so it is a fraction of that). Only the player actually changes, so caching the static casters would be an easy win if frame time becomes an issue.
10. **Washed goo never comes back.** Twelve splats is the whole game: once they are gone there is nothing left to aim at, and `R` respawns the player, not the world. A respawn timer (or a refill on respawn) closes the loop.
11. **The gamepad can't spray.** Right trigger sprints and `A` jumps; the jet is mouse or `F` only.
12. **Piers and boats have no horizontal collision.** The deck is walkable through `World.pierHeight`, but the pilings are not `Obstacle`s, so you can walk or swim through a post, and a boat can be driven through the jetty and through the other three boats (`resolveObstacles` is skipped while riding). Adding post obstacles and a pier-deck test into `Boat.update`'s bow sample would close both.
13. **A boat stays wherever it is left.** There is no docking: it does not snap back to its pier, and there is no way to call one from another island, so stranding a boat on a cay is permanent for that session.
14. **Distant islands cast no shadows**: the shadow box follows the player, so anything outside 90 m of them is unlit by the map.
15. No tests. The repo is public at https://github.com/DennisFaucher/OpenWorld (v5.0 released).
16. **Sound has only been judged by ear once.** The first listen found the surf drowning the footsteps and deep water silent; the bed is now 6 dB quieter, the footsteps louder and swimming has strokes. Everything is still measured rather than heard — whether the strokes read as swimming, whether the deposit bells climb pleasantly, and whether the jet is too loud remain open, and `--audio-dump DIR` writes every buffer to disk for exactly this.
17. **Synthesising at launch costs about a third of a second** before the window appears (12 s of stereo surf is most of it). Parallelising the buffers across cores, or generating the bed lazily once the window is up, would hide it.
18. **Nothing reacts to the shrine.** All forty crystals light the tower and speed the pack's refill, and that is the whole of it: the islanders do not gather, no dialogue acknowledges it, and the shrine cannot be un-lit. The payoff is a place to look at, not an event.
19. **There is nothing to go inside.** The plaza buildings are solid boxes with painted doors; the tower is the only interactive thing in the world.

## Roadmap

Ordered roughly by value-to-effort. Each item should end with updated `shots/` renders and a note here.

### M1: Look and feel polish
- [x] **Real-time shadows** (directional shadow map, PCF or soft edges); replace the blob shadow with a contact-shadow fallback
  - Single 4096² depth map, orthographic light frustum ±160, hardware PCF (`sample_compare`, 3x3 grid), receiver-plane offset along the geometric normal (0.25) plus a slope-scaled bias. The light camera sits at `+sunDir * 300` (it used to be `-sunDir * 300`, which rendered the map from *under* the island and produced no usable shadows).
- [x] Soft bloom + subtle tone mapping / color grading pass for the "sunny" look
  - MSAA HDR (`rgba16Float`) scene target resolves into a half-res bright pass (threshold 1.0, knee 0.5), two ping-pong 9-tap Gaussian blurs, then a composite that adds bloom at 0.30 and applies a highlight-only soft-knee tone curve (identity below 0.86) with a mild saturation lift. ACES was tried first and washed the display-space palette out; the knee curve keeps the authored colours intact. Shaded surfaces get a small additive sky fill so shadows read cool without going muddy.
  - Water deliberately does **not** receive shadows: at grazing angles the shadow-map texel footprint blows up and the island silhouette appeared as hard bands offshore.
  - Composite exposure is trimmed to 0.92 before the tone curve (added with the terrain-detail work) to leave highlight headroom.
- [x] **Better terrain texturing** and plaza tile art
  - The vertex colour still carries the broad material blend (sand -> grass -> rock, by height then slope); `fs_main` modulates it with `fbm` patches plus two `vnoise` octaves for grain, a shallow dune ripple on sand, damp/darker sand near the water line, and low-contrast blotches on rock. Detail fades to its mean through `aaNoise` once an octave gets finer than a pixel, so far ground dissolves instead of shimmering.
  - **Footprint caveat:** `fwidth(wp.xz)` is very anisotropic on flat ground seen at a shallow angle. Summing both derivatives faded detail that was still resolvable, so the LOD uses `max(fwidth(p).x, fwidth(p).y)`.
  - Height-based rock strata were tried and reverted: they read as topographic contour lines.
  - Plaza: per-tile tone from `hash21(cell)` and anti-aliased grout (fixed 4%-of-tile `step` before, which shivered at distance).
  - Composite exposure trimmed to 0.92 before the knee. Lit grass and sand sat at the top of the curve, which was flattening ground detail and blowing out the wet sand.
  - Rock is now a light warm grey (was `0.64, 0.54, 0.42`) and only starts on steeper faces (`0.74 -> 0.52` vs `n.y`); the old colour made the hill shoulder look like a mud slide.
- [x] Underwater look when the camera dips below the surface (fog tint, caustics)
  - `uwAmount(camY)` crossfades 0 -> 1 over `+0.30 .. -0.10`, so the camera passing through the surface blends instead of popping. Three shaders branch on it: `applyFog` (dense teal absorption at 0.065/m, versus 0.0035/m in air), `fs_sky` (open water below the horizon), and `fs_water` (the surface seen from below).
  - Caustics: `fs_main` adds a ridged-noise web to anything with `wp.y < 0` and an upward normal, fading with depth. Only evaluated when submerged.
  - Under the surface the water is a bright rippling ceiling inside the Snell window (~49 degrees from vertical); steeper grazing angles fall back to total internal reflection and go dark.
  - **The camera could never reach the water before.** `updateCamera` clamped it to `y >= 0.5` unconditionally, so the item was unreachable. It now clamps against the seabed while swimming, and the pitch limit went from -0.1 to -0.55 so you can dive the camera by pitching down in open water. On land it still never goes below the waterline.
  - Everything underwater decays to the single colour `UWFOG`. An earlier version varied the fog target with the fragment's own height, which made the distant seabed and the distant surface converge to different colours and drew a hard dark bar along the far waterline.
- [x] Water refraction/depth-fog improvements; wake and splash particles when the player moves in water
  - **Refraction** is done in the depth lookup rather than the image: the seabed height is re-sampled at `wp.xz + N.xz * (0.35 + depth * 0.55)`, so the shoreline and the shallow shelf wobble with the waves. A real screen-space refraction would need a second scene pass, which the single-pass layout does not have.
  - `Particles.swift` is a CPU pool of ~600 sprites (`--walk` renders them headlessly). Foam is pinned to `y = 0.05` and spreads; droplets get gravity. The renderer rebuilds them as camera-facing quads each frame into one dynamic buffer, drawn after the water with depth test but no depth write.
  - The particle pass is skipped entirely while the camera is submerged: the water plane is opaque from below but writes no depth, so spray above the surface would otherwise punch through it.
  - **Play-test fixes.** The entry splash never fired in practice: walking off a beach into deep water is a continuous seabed, so the terrain snap zeroes `vel.y` every frame and the `fallSpeed < -3.5` test could only ever see a real jump-drop. It now uses `max(-fallSpeed, speed * 0.6)` and has a cooldown. The wake also read as a trail of bubbles — a soft round sprite does that no matter the tint — so foam is squashed into an ellipse, faded at grazing angles, and broken up with world-space noise in `fs_particle`.
  - **Buoyancy was launching the player out of the water.** The spring used the raw displacement, so landing even a metre deep accelerated at ~150 m/s^2, throwing the player clear; every bob back in re-triggered the splash, which is what painted the sea with white smears. The displacement is now clamped to +/-0.9 m with stronger damping.
  - Two water shader knock-ons turned up while chasing that: depth was measured from the displaced surface (`wp.y`) rather than mean sea level, so the shallow band and its foam tracked the wave crests, and the fine breakup noise had to replace a 2.2/m one that read as cloud-shaped smears. Clouds are also excluded from the water reflection, where the wave normals shredded their edges.
- [x] Sky: moving cloud shadows, optional time-of-day parameter (sun direction/color)
  - One cloud field (`cloudField`) at altitude 260. The sky intersects it along the view ray, so the clouds converge towards the horizon; `fs_main` projects the *same* field along the sun, so the shadow crossing the island belongs to the cloud overhead.
  - `--hour` swings the sun on an arc that passes exactly through the original hand-picked direction at 12:00. `Renderer.sun(hour:)` also returns the light colour (warming to `1.0, 0.52, 0.28` at the horizon), which now travels in the frame uniform (`f.sunCol`) instead of the old `SUNCOL` constant. The ambient dims and warms with `L.y`, and `horizonColor` warms the sky and the distance fog together.
  - A full sunset (17:30+) is dim and desaturated on the ground — physically right, since there is no bounce light, but it is the weakest part of the range.
- [x] **Shorelines + foliage shading** (known issues 2 and 5)
  - Two-sided lighting now blends between the front/back diffuse terms instead of hard-flipping the normal, which removed the dotted dark seam that appeared where the ground was tangent to the view (most obvious along shorelines). Water shallows fade in over a wider band (`exp(-depth*0.30)`, alpha `0.20 + depth*0.30`) and the foam band is wider, so the shore is a gradient rather than a hard cut. Palm fronds carry a translucency flag so their undersides glow rather than going black.
- [x] Fix known issues 3-4 above
  - Stone kerb around the plaza, ambient/face term for shaded walls, contact-AO gradient baked into building walls. See issues 3 and 4.

### M2: Character and movement
- [x] Proper movement feel: variable jump height, coyote time, jump buffering, heavier falling
  - Variable height cuts `vel.y` by 0.42 on release, so a tap is a 0.58 m hop and a hold is a 2.43 m jump off the same button. Coyote (0.12 s) and the buffer (0.12 s) cover presses made just after leaving / just before landing, and falling uses 42 m/s² against 30 rising so the arc does not float.
  - Measured with `--sim`: apex 2.43 m held vs 0.58 m tapped, 0.70 s of air, and a press 0.07 s before touchdown fires on the landing frame instead of being swallowed.
  - **Coyote time is barely exercised.** Slope handling below removed the worst of the glue, and walking off the rocky outcrop does now produce airborne frames, but every drop on this island ends in the sea within a frame or two — and swimming makes jumping legal anyway. The window is real code waiting for cliffs, which M4 brings.
- [x] Better character model and animation (idle, run, jump, fall, swim strokes), still original design
  - Rebuilt as a real rig in `Player.swift`: hips, chest, head and hat, plus two segments and a hand or foot per limb, drawn from overlapping sphere primitives. Same character (straw hat, red band, blue shirt, cream trousers, brown shoes), now about 20 parts instead of a torso, a head and four sticks.
  - Six states — idle, run, rise, fall, swim, slide — each a pure function of the walk phase and time. Weights are blended *between states* rather than between joint angles: a 10/s low-pass on a 2 Hz stride flattened the legs to a shuffle (hip swing came out at 29° instead of 46°). Transitions still ease because the weights ease.
  - Airborne weight cross-fades on `vel.y` rather than switching at the apex, so the jump does not pop between rise and fall. The stride rate is now `min(speed, 11) * 1.5` — the old 2.6 multiplier was about twice a believable cadence.
  - Rig gotchas worth keeping: `out` is a Z rotation in the torso frame, so the same sign that abducts a hanging arm adducts a raised one (the rise pose uses a negative value to spread the arms). Limb angles are positive forward, which is why every joint applies `rotationX(-angle)`.
  - Verified headlessly with `--jump` on the shot path and `--look RAD`, which re-orbits the finished frame so a run cycle can be seen from the side: stride extremes at frames 38 and 50 of a `--walk` (hip ±45.8°, knee 11–63°), tucked-leg V-armed rise, arms-out fall, horizontal swim with the head and back above the surface, braced slide.
- [x] Footstep dust / sand / water splash particles
  - A third particle kind (`Particle.kind`: 0 droplet, 1 foam, 2 dust) rather than inferring the look from gravity, which was standing in for "is this foam". Dust gets its own high-frequency noise breakup in `fs_particle` and a soft edge; foam keeps the low-frequency one.
  - Emission: one puff per foot contact (the stride advances by pi, so a change in the step index is the contact, alternating feet), a wider cloud on landing from a fall, a continuous skid while sliding, and a scuff when the player presses into a face too steep to climb. The last one closes the loop on the slope work: a silent stall read as "the game is stuck", and now it reads as digging in.
  - Tint comes from the ground it left — sand, dry grass or rock via the same masks as `World.buildTerrain` — lightened 35% toward white, because a dust cloud is always paler than the surface. Dry ground only: the shallows already spray.
  - Gotcha: on a steep face a puff spawned at the feet is *inside* the terrain and never draws, because it is depth-tested against the ground it just left. The skid cloud is lifted and given upward velocity for that reason.
  - Verified headlessly: sand puffs trailing a run along the beach, a scuff cloud at the foot of the scarp while holding forward, a skid trail on the 48° face, an impact ring on landing on the plaza, nothing at all while idle.
- [x] Camera polish: auto-recenter behind movement, look-ahead, FOV kick while running
  - Look-ahead offsets the boom pivot along the run by up to 1.0 m, faded out as the camera pulls in. Auto-recenter only runs after 1.2 s without camera input and only while pushing forward — recentring during a strafe would rotate the frame the strafe is measured in. The FOV kick is +3.6° at a sprint, eased in over about half a second.
  - Chasing the look-ahead turned up a real bug: the boom floor was 1.5 m, but the pivot is pushed forward, so a pulled-in camera sat inside the player's head (reproduce with `--walk-fast` from the spawn, frame 60). The floor is now 2.2 m and the look-ahead fades below 2.5 m.
- [x] Slope handling: slide on steep slopes instead of walking up cliffs
  - The vertical snap lifted the player onto whatever was under them, so any face was climbable at full speed. A step into ground past the walk limit now keeps only the component that runs along the contour, and standing on such ground removes traction entirely: `Game.slideAccel` 34 m/s² down the fall line against `slideDrag` 2/s, whatever the input says.
  - 45° (`Game.walkSlopeY`) is measured rather than guessed — `--slope-scan` histograms the terrain gradient. The island peaks at 65° around the rocky outcrop at (55, -38); 1% of the sampled area is past 45°, and the mountain flank tops out at 44°, so the one landmark stays climbable.
  - Verified headlessly: walking west into the outcrop from the shore climbs 38° → 45° and then stops advancing in x, sliding along the face instead; starting on a 54° patch with forward held slides down the fall line from y 3.27 to the waterline, `grounded` never dropping; the 44° mountain flank still walks at full pace.
  - Play-test spawns: `--pos 60 -30 --yaw 1.571` stands you at the foot of the scarp, where holding forward climbs to 44.5° and stalls; `--pos 59 -29 --yaw 1.571` starts you on a 52° face, which slides immediately.
  - Emergent result worth keeping: the mountain flank crosses 45° on some bearings, so the direct line to the summit is blocked and the player has to switchback up it. That reads as terrain rather than as an obstacle, which is the argument for keeping the limit at 45° instead of loosening it to keep every route open.

### M3: "Spray pack" mechanic (original water-jet tool)
- [x] Hold-to-spray water jet: nozzle in the right hand, ballistic stream cut at the impact, splash, and a wet patch that dries (`Spray.swift`)
  - Particles stayed on the CPU. The plan said GPU, but at ~1200 live droplets the integration is a fraction of a millisecond and the billboard expansion was the only real cost, so the existing path was kept and the pool raised from 600 to 2400. A compute system only pays off an order of magnitude above this.
- [x] Hover variant with a limited tank that refills in water
  - Thrust only above 42 m/s², the falling gravity, or the pack cannot break contact with the ground at all; the climb rate is capped so it reads as a hover rather than a launch. The tank refills at `0.85`/s while swimming or wading, with a 25% re-arm threshold so the jet does not stutter at empty, and the tank mesh on the back shrinks with the level.
  - One drain rate was wrong for both jobs: washing a splat and lifting yourself cost the same, so a tank long enough to wash with was a jetpack. The drain is now `0.07`/s with an extra `0.26`/s once the aim is more than ~25° down — about fourteen seconds of washing or three of lift.
- [x] Aiming, reticle and tank gauge
  - Fixed after a play-test: the world bound was a hard-coded 320 m circle, five metres inside the north-east island's beach, so a crystal and the island's whole outer quarter sat behind an invisible wall in the middle of dry land — you bounced off the air walking to one. The bound is now derived from the islands (374 m), the sea floor sheet and the shore-depth texture reach further than it, `Shards.field` refuses to place a crystal within 6 m of it, and `--shard-scan` flags any that end up out of reach. Verified: walking the same line that used to stall at exactly r 320 now crosses it and picks the crystal up.
  - The reticle used to draw whenever the eye ray hit anything, which is always: a thin ring at the middle of the screen reads as a force field around whatever it happens to sit on (reported on a gem across a bay). It now fades in with `sprayBlend`, so it belongs to the stream.
  - The first version fired along the camera direction. With 5.5 m/s² of gravity that lands metres short on any downward aim, so the stream fell visibly short of the splat it was pointed at and the only way to hit anything was to walk into range and guess. `Spray.aim` now marches the eye ray and solves the elevation that carries the arc *through* the point under the middle of the screen (flatter of the two solutions), so the water goes where you look.
  - A world-space marker on the ground was tried first and never appeared: the camera looks at the player, so the aim point is always hidden behind their body. The marker is a screen-space ring instead, which is exactly truthful once the arc is solved to the eye-ray hit, and it costs nothing — it is drawn in the composite pass. The boom anchor also moved from 1.5 m to 2.15 m so the character sits low in frame and the ground being aimed at is visible past them.
  - The tank bar lives in the same composite pass (pixel-space rounded rect, dark frame, fill from the bottom, amber under 25%) — no extra pass, no HUD geometry, no text. Verified headlessly at full, ~15% and empty.
- [x] Session readout beside the tank gauge: steps, miles and calories
  - Numbers are formatted on the CPU into character codes and the shader only draws glyphs, so there is no digit-splitting in MSL and no field width to get wrong in two places. The font is 5x6, one bit per pixel, packed into a `uint` per glyph; 4x6 was tried first and letters were unreadable (`M` read as `H`).
  - The cell size snaps to a whole number of pixels and the glyph edges are feathered by half a pixel: at 0.9 px per font pixel the strokes aliased into dashed lines at 1280x720. Verified at 1920x1080, 1280x720 and a 4x crop of both.
  - Steps come from the walk phase (the same footfall that fires the dust) rather than a timer, so the count matches the animation; distance only accumulates while grounded and not swimming, and calories are `steps * 0.045`. A session long enough to overflow the four-cell fields clips rather than overlapping the next column.
- [x] Interactable targets: twelve goo splats on the plaza and the paths that the jet washes away
  - Ground only. Splats are lobed quads drawn in the particle buffer (`kind` 5), shrinking as their health drops; the splash is tinted green while a splat is being washed. Walls would need a second quad orientation and are left for later. A splat counts as hit within `radius + 0.35` m, because by the time the jet lands the water has already spread over the rim.

### M4: World scale and streaming

Shipped as v4.0 with three items deliberately deferred: true terrain streaming (the heightmap is still one mesh built at launch, just chunked and culled), shadow cascades, and bridges. All three are recorded in Known issues.
- [x] Chunked terrain with frustum culling
  - The 240-unit grid is cut into 15x15 chunks of 16 m, each a contiguous index range into the one shared vertex buffer, with a box taken from the heights it owns. `Frustum` extracts six clip planes from the frame's view-projection and a chunk is skipped when its box is outside any of them. Measured with the new `--stats` flag: 55 of 225 chunks (28k of 115k triangles) from the plaza spawn, 205 from the 110 m overview, where the whole island really is in view.
  - The shadow pass uses the same routine against the light's orthographic projection. It culls almost nothing today because the shadow frustum is a fixed +-160 box around the island; tying it to the island's bounds (or to the camera) is the next easy win.
- [x] Archipelago: several islands instead of one, with the height function and prop placement per island
  - `World.islands` is four entries: the home island keeps its centre and radius, so the plaza, the mountain and every landmark are untouched; three cays at (-200, 130), (180, 214) and (96, -204) use the same profile with their own radius, summit and noise seed. `height` is a max over them, and each island returns the sea floor immediately when the sample is more than `radius + 40` away, which is what keeps the build cheap: the whole world builds in about half a second, including props.
  - Each island is its own square patch at 1 m, sized to clear the wobbliest coastline (radius + 26) and rounded up to a whole number of chunks. Patches never overlap — the nearest pair still leaves an 8 m gap — so the flat outer ring of each patch meets the sea-floor sheet exactly at the sea floor height with no z-fighting, because the sheet has the patch rectangles cut out of it.
  - Palms, bushes and flowers are placed per island, counts scaled by radius. The buildings and the bell tower stay on the home island.
  - The shadow box now follows the player (90 m ortho, snapped to whole shadow texels along the light's axes) instead of covering a fixed +-160 m around the origin. A 4096 map across 680 m would be 5 texels per metre; this keeps the old density wherever the player is standing. Distant islands therefore cast no shadows — cascades are the fix, and are not done.
  - Distance fog was halved (`0.0035` to `0.0022`) because at the old density the far islands were pale smudges at 300 m. The home island's horizon is a little crisper as a result.
  - Swimming across open water was checked with `--sim`: steady 3.9 m/s, no falling through the sea floor, bounds at 320 m.
- [x] LOD: coarser index ranges for distant chunks, so a chunk is 512 triangles near and fewer far
  - Each chunk now carries three index ranges over the same vertices: full 1 m (512 triangles), every other vertex (128) and every fourth (32). Levels are chosen **per island patch**, from the distance between the camera and the patch box: under 70 m full, under 190 m half, beyond that quarter.
  - Per patch rather than per chunk because a chunk whose neighbour is at a different level shares an edge at a different resolution — the coarse side is a straight line where the fine side has a vertex, and the gap shows as a bright sliver against the water. A patch's only neighbour is the flat sea floor at exactly `seabed`, and every patch edge vertex is at `seabed` at any level, so switching a whole island cannot crack. The cost is that the home island is all-or-nothing: standing on the north beach, its south side is still 1 m.
  - Measured with `--stats`: spawn 123 chunks / 33.8k triangles (was 63.0k), beach 26.6k (was ~63k), 140 m overview 103.8k (was 186.4k). From a 330 m aerial the home island is half and the far cays quarter. Compared crops of the same view at 1 m and 2 m: indistinguishable apart from a slightly softer ridge line.
  - Small props are cut by distance as well: flowers past 150 m and bushes past 260 m are skipped per batch, since a 0.28 m flower is a sub-pixel speck that still costs 48 triangles. The 140 m overview drops from 834 to 288 instances.
- [ ] Heightmap from tiles/noise per chunk instead of one global mesh; stream by player position
- [x] Instanced rendering for palms/bushes/flowers
  - `World.buildProps` returns the static structures (houses, tower, kerb) as one merged mesh plus `PropBatch` lists: a `PropKind` (three palm shapes, or the unit sphere that bushes and flowers scale), an array of instance transforms, and a culling box. Instances are bucketed into 40 m world blocks, so a batch is a handful of palms rather than a whole island — from the plaza, 41 of 115 batches and 357 of 944 instances are submitted (it was all of them, in both passes).
  - New `vs_inst`/`vs_shadow_inst` vertex functions and `mainInstPSO`/`shadowInstPSO`, with the per-instance buffer at index 3 (0 vertices, 1 Frame, 2 Obj). Tint rides in the instance instead of `Obj.tint`, so `fs_main` is unchanged.
  - Palms now vary by yaw and by a non-uniform scale (height and a little width) around a base mesh built at 7 m, instead of being baked one by one from the seed. Frond shape is fixed per variant; `--stats` prints the batch and instance counts.
- [ ] Shadow cascades, so a distant island casts the shadows it should
- [x] Bigger water extent with a stable clip-map grid
  - The water was one camera-centred grid with quadratic spacing: its vertices slid with the camera, so the vertex waves swam. It is now a clipmap — nine 64x64 rings, `1 << level` metres per cell, from 1 m out to ±8 km — and every level is snapped to a **single** origin on whole metres. That anchors the vertices to the world: crossing a metre re-covers the same points with the same lattice, so the surface does not move at all. Water triangles drop from 180k to 57k in nine draws.
  - One origin for all levels, not one each. Snapping each level to its own cell size looks tighter, but then a ring's hole and the finer square it overlaps disagree by up to a cell: a strip up to 1 m wide loses its water on one side and double-blends it on the other, and that strip travels with the camera. It is nearly invisible in deep water (the seabed behind it is the same dark blue) and obvious in the shallows, which is exactly the case that matters.
  - Ring seams therefore land exactly on top of each other, and there is no stitching: at the 32 m boundary the fine and coarse edges occupy the same points in xz, and the vertex waves there differ by under a centimetre.
  - Verified with renders from a beach, a grazing angle, underwater and straight down over a shoreline, plus a fractional player position (the misalignment bug showed up only at non-integer camera positions, which is why integer test spots hid it). "No swimming" is a property of the construction — fixed vertex world positions — not something a still can show.
- [x] Boats and piers
  - One jetty per island, sited by walking out from the island centre to the waterline and then to the drop-off, so nothing is hand-placed and nothing moves when an island moves. Planks are laid along the pier's own axes as quads, because the box helper is axis-aligned and a rotated jetty built from boxes is not an option.
  - The deck is a prop but the player's vertical snap asks `World.pierHeight` about it, so the pier is walkable without the terrain mesh (or the water-depth texture) knowing it exists. The snap only consults the deck when the player is already above it, otherwise swimming under the pier would pop them up through the planks.
  - The boat is a 90-triangle hull with a painted stripe, driven by the same stick the legs use: forward is throttle, sideways is steer. Speed eases toward 12 m/s, reverse is 4.5 m/s, and the rudder gets *better* with way on (0.55 rad/s at rest to 1.1 at speed) while inverting when backing up. Crossing to the nearest cay is about ten seconds instead of twenty swimming.
  - The first version could not come about: 0.22 rad/s at rest is fourteen seconds for a U-turn, and the shallow test blocked on the bow *and* the stern, so a hull nosed into a beach was stuck there with reverse, W and the rudder all doing nothing. Found in play, not headlessly. Now only the leading end is sampled in the direction of travel, so a bow-in boat can back out and a stern-in boat can drive away (a 15-second `--sim` with throttle and steering held no longer parks itself against the shore).
  - Boarding is one edge-triggered key that takes the nearest boat within 3.4 m or steps off the one being ridden, so there is no way to get stuck aboard. The tank does not refill from the surface a hand's width under the hull.
  - The key is prompted on screen (`E BOARD` in reach, `E LEAVE` aboard a stopped boat) because the first play-test ended with "oh, I need to press E". It rides in the spare `gauge.w` slot, so the prompt cost no new uniform, and five glyphs joined the bitmap font.
  - The boat's wake is foam only (`ParticleSystem.wake`), two arms peeling off the stern: droplets thrown up would arc through the air and read as a fountain. Verified with renders moored, under way from behind and from the side, stopped in the shallows, and a 25-second `--sim` for the rudder and the shallow-water stop. The boarding toggle was exercised with `--board` (E held) from the pier, not with a keyboard.

### M5: Gameplay layer
- [x] Collectibles and save state
  - Forty crystals, placed by rejection sampling against `World.height`: dry land, ground walkable at 45 deg with a margin, nine metres apart, clear of walls and not on a jetty. Shares per island are proportional to area, so the home island gets 18 and the smallest cay 5. Indices are stable because the field is seeded, which is what lets the save file store indices instead of coordinates.
  - An eight-triangle bipyramid with per-face normals, spun and bobbed from the clock, drawn with a tint above 1 so the bloom pass picks it up. A soft halo sprite is emitted behind each gem from the particle pass — without it a crystal reads as another flower at 40 m, even in gold.
  - Walking into one takes it: no key, because a prompt would turn collecting into a chore. A burst of kind-6 sparkles (four-point stars) and a counter in a pill above the activity readout. A row of the bitmap font is four characters, so "12/40" needs two registers — hence `shards` plus `shardTail`.
  - Progress is a `SaveState` in `~/Library/Application Support/OpenWorld/save.json`, written on every pickup and on quit, and keyed to a hash of the field so a moved crystal drops the file instead of marking the wrong one taken. Headless runs are hermetic: they neither read nor write it without `--save PATH`.
  - Verified: the field (`--shard-scan`, 40 shards), a pickup and the sparkle (a `--sim` walk with the take printed, then a render mid-burst), the save round trip (pick up, exit, relaunch, `1/40` restored), the counter at 0, 1, 39 and 40 (the last in gold), and that found crystals are not drawn. Not verified live: the file being written on window close.
- [ ] NPCs / simple AI wanderers, a few interactive buildings
  - [x] Villagers. Six of them, using the player's rig in different clothes (`Look`) and the same stride functions (`PoseClock`), so a villager is 20 spheres and no new pipeline. They walk between waypoints drawn from the terrain within a home radius, refuse a step that would leave the land, turn towards the player inside 4.5 m, stop, and wave for the first couple of seconds. Idle and walk are blended by speed rather than switched at a threshold. Hand-placed homes (plaza, two beaches, the south slope, the east meadow, the big cay), because these are characters with places to be. Drawn in the shadow pass as well as the scene pass.
  - Verified: `--villager-scan` for their starts (all on ground under 31 deg), a 900-frame `--sim` with zero villagers ending below sea level, and renders of one walking, one waving on the plaza, one on the cay. Not verified live: whether the wave reads as friendly rather than odd at play speed.
  - [ ] Interactive buildings (doors that open, a building you can enter)
- [x] Sound: ambient surf and wind, birds, footsteps, splash, the jet, the outboard, the pickup chime
  - Every buffer is synthesised at launch (`Sound.swift`) and played by `AVAudioPlayerNode`s (`Audio.swift`); there are no asset files, and nothing is generated in the render callback. The bed is four looping nodes; the one-shots pool across ten.
  - Surf is two bands of filtered noise under a swell of two slow LFOs that complete whole cycles in the twelve-second loop, so the loop crossfade (`Sound.seamless`) blends identical modulation. The first version had a swell floor of 0.04, which raised to the 2.4 power silenced the sea for two seconds in every twelve; the floor is now 0.68 and the exponent gentler.
  - The jet hisses while the stream is live, the outboard goes through a varispeed unit whose rate follows hull speed, footfalls are three variants at random volume, and a pickup rings three bell notes up an A major triad (A5, C#6, E6) built from struck-bar partials rather than harmonics. The surf is loudest at the shoreline and the wind on the ridge, both from `World.height` under the player.
  - Events are *inferred*: the step counter advancing is a footfall, `found` growing is a pickup, `swimming`/`onGround`/`riding` transitions are the splash, the landing and the engine. Nothing in `Game` changed, so sound failing leaves the game identical.
  - Underwater is one low-pass across the mix at 420 Hz with +9 dB of make-up gain, crossfaded on the shader's own `uwAmount(camY)` curve, so the ear and the eye agree about where the surface is. Without the gain it measured 13 dB quieter than the beach, which is a mute rather than a muffle.
  - First listen found the bed drowning the foley: the surf masked footsteps and wading splashes entirely, and deep water was silent apart from the entry splash. The bed is now 6 dB quieter (surf 0.10 inland to 0.42 at the shoreline, wind 0.04 to 0.22), the footsteps are louder (0.45-0.62), and swimming has strokes of its own — a band sweeping up and down through the pull with a low gulp behind it, fired off the walk phase, which is the one thing that keeps advancing in deep water because `steps` deliberately does not. Measured after the change: a footstep peaks 9.8x above the bed floor in the recorded mix, a stroke 7.7x.
  - Verified: `--audio-scan` for levels and loop seams (wrap step below the local p99 for all four loops), `--audio-test` which plays the real graph through a scripted session, records the mix and asserts on it (land RMS 0.084 / brightness 0.49, low-pass 0.065 / 0.01, underwater 0.083 / 0.018, outboard at level, peaks under 0.63), the chime present as a transient in the recording, and the buffers dumped as CAF for listening.
  - **Not verified: how any of it sounds.** Levels, seams and the filter can be measured; taste cannot. The dumps in `--audio-dump DIR` are the thing to listen to.
  - No music. A bed of looped pads would fight the ambience and can only be judged by ear, so it is deliberately absent rather than half-done.
- [x] Simple quest/goal loop so there is something to *do*
  - The bell tower on the plaza is the shrine. Walking up to it with crystals offers them, one every 0.085 s with no keypress: the walk is the gesture. Ten lamps spiral up the shaft, four crystals each, drawn as glowing billboards above the bloom threshold so they read from the far side of the plaza; all forty light a column of light off the spire, which is the only thing in the world visible from another island. The reward is a pack that refills nearly three times as fast. The delivered count is saved (optionally, so pre-shrine files still load) and the HUD pill keeps quoting the field, because a counter that sits at 0 while you carry a handful reads as broken. Verified headlessly: `--sim 400 --shrine 40 --pos -8 16` lights all ten lamps and reports SHRINE COMPLETE; nothing is delivered standing at spawn; a save round-trips `banked`; `--audio-test` captures the bells and asserts all forty arrive.
  - Not done: nothing reacts to it beyond the tower and the refill rate. The islanders do not gather, and the shrine cannot be un-lit.
